# Three Business Hub — Group Operating Plan

Last updated: 11 November 2026

This is the controlling operating plan for the three separate businesses. Keep each business's contacts, records, finances and permissions separate. Share a lead between businesses only where relevant and lawful, with appropriate permission. Apply these instructions consistently; do not ask the owner to repeat settled business-model details.

## Group structure

- **Kiely Industrial Coatings (K.I.C.)** — industrial coatings, repainting and refurbishment. K.I.C. undertakes straightforward, manageable hands-on coating jobs itself, including suitable skip and container work. Larger, specialist, high-risk or capacity-heavy projects may be delivered using independent subcontractors.
- **CoatMatch UK** — specialist subcontractor discovery, verification and work-package introductions. Build both a genuine contractor supply network and a buyer/work-package network.
- **Pipeline Pilot AI UK** — prospect research and sales-pipeline support. Validate the initial service and pricing before making claims or selling.

## K.I.C. commercial strategy

- K.I.C. is a hybrid hands-on and subcontracted delivery business, not solely a managing agent.
- K.I.C. directly undertakes straightforward coating and repainting work it is competent and properly equipped to perform, especially suitable skips, RoRo containers, bins and other accessible/easy steel items.
- Larger, specialist, complex, high-access, safety-critical or capacity-heavy jobs can be priced and delivered with independent subcontractors. Do not assume every job is subcontracted, and do not assume every job is self-delivered.
- K.I.C. manages enquiries, customer relationships, surveys/briefing as agreed, specifications, quotations, contracts, work planning, quality evidence and handover. For self-delivered jobs, K.I.C. also carries out the physical application work.
- Do not claim employees, owned plant, premises, vehicles, accreditations, references or completed projects unless verified.
- Owner's current cash-flow preference: do not purchase or activate insurance policies before there is a realistic job/revenue opportunity and the cost is justified. Obtain and compare quotations and clarify cover requirements without accepting or paying for a policy prematurely.
- **Critical start-work gate:** the cash-flow preference does not authorise uninsured work. Before accepting or commencing any job, check the customer's contractual requirements and applicable legal duties, and put appropriate insurance in force for the actual activities and delivery model. If suitable cover is not active, do not start physical work; seek a deposit, staged payment, customer-funded mobilisation or a properly insured subcontract route instead. Never tell a buyer K.I.C. is insured until documentary evidence confirms it.
- For subcontracted work, verify the subcontractor's relevant insurance, competence, RAMS, COSHH and access controls; ensure contracts allocate responsibilities and insurance requirements correctly.
- Customer deposits and staged payments are preferred where negotiated, especially to fund materials, labour and mobilisation; never assume a public buyer will pay upfront.
- Target gross project margin: **25%**. Required selling price for a 25% gross margin = total direct project cost / 0.75.
- Quote easy skip/container jobs as practical, clearly scoped packages. Include preparation, coating system, materials, labour, transport, access, waste controls, drying/curing, exclusions and payment terms. Do not promise a price before scope and costs are known.

### K.I.C. delivery and bid gates

Before any bid, quotation or commitment, check exact scope/specification; site survey/access; coating system and surface preparation; labour and subcontractor quotations where relevant; materials; equipment/access hire; transport; waste/wastewater controls; insurance; competence/training; RAMS/COSHH; environmental controls; programme; payment terms; retention; bonds; working capital; contingency; and 25% margin.

Do not start physical work until the applicable insurance and safety requirements are satisfied, site controls are agreed, and written customer/subcontract terms are signed. Welding, structural repairs, lifting and safety-critical work require suitably competent specialists.

## CoatMatch UK

Build both sides of the network:
- **Supply:** specialist industrial painters, protective-coating applicators, roof/cladding teams, concrete repair specialists, steelwork and other relevant subcontractors.
- **Demand:** principal contractors, industrial maintenance firms, FM providers, asset owners, fabricators, waste/container operators and property managers with identifiable work packages.

Capture trade, geography, capacity, availability, evidence of experience, insurance and relevant competence documents, verification status, official contact route, work-package fit and introduction history. Never describe a contractor as vetted/insured/accredited until documentary checks are complete. Obtain contractor permission before sharing their details or making introductions. Respect opt-outs and avoid duplicate outreach. Test qualified introductions and willingness to pay before investing in expensive marketplace development.

## Pipeline Pilot AI UK

Start with a simple, deliverable service offer to validate:
- Targeted B2B prospect research and company/contact lists.
- CRM and sales-pipeline setup/clean-up.
- Lead qualification and prioritisation.
- Follow-up reminders and preparation of outreach drafts.
- Lightweight sales-process/AI workflow setup only where the capability is actually available.

Before outreach, define deliverables, exclusions, onboarding information, pricing, time/cost to fulfil, data sources and lawful basis. Do not claim proprietary software, automated integrations, guaranteed lead volumes, conversion rates or revenue unless built and verified. Interview prospective SME customers and validate willingness to pay. Keep first offers small and measurable; do not sell unvalidated features.

## Shared hub record structure

Maintain separate business records for:
- Sales pipeline / opportunities.
- CRM contacts and organisations.
- Contractor network and verification.
- Projects and jobs.
- Quotations and cost breakdowns.
- Tasks and follow-ups.
- Activity log and source evidence.

Suggested stages: Researching → Ready to contact → Contacted → Replied → Qualified → Quotation → Won/Lost. Drafts are not sent messages; sent does not mean delivered or read; no revenue is counted until evidenced. Deduplicate by organisation, opportunity, buyer, notice/reference and email history before adding or contacting.

## Group reporting

Weekly report should separate:
- New researched opportunities.
- Verified prospects and contractors.
- Drafts prepared.
- Messages actually sent.
- Replies, bounces and opt-outs.
- Qualified enquiries.
- Quotations.
- Won/lost work.
- Actual revenue and gross profit, when evidenced.
- Upcoming deadlines and owner decisions.

No automatic tender submission, acceptance of work, purchases, financial commitments or account changes without the owner's explicit approval.
